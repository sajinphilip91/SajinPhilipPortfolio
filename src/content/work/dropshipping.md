## 1. Understand

### Four problems, one retailer

Spocket is a dropshipping app that connects to Shopify and WooCommerce. Unlike competitors focused on AliExpress, its suppliers are mostly in the United States, Europe and Australia, which keeps shipping times shorter.

The team identified four problems on the Order and Search pages:

1. There is no clear way for customers to track their packages and orders on the orders page.
2. It's difficult for retailers to narrow down searches.
3. It's difficult for retailers to narrow down their orders.
4. It's difficult for retailers to contact Spocket about order issues.

> How might we help retailers find the right product and trust what happens after they order?

## 3. Ideate

### What the design should appeal to

Commerce is emotional. I considered triggers including urgency, belonging, curiosity and trust.

### Why, when and how people filter

- **Why?** They can't find the exact product they're looking for.
- **When?** After finding the product.
- **How?** Through the filter button and a pop-up.

Filtering is a recovery step, so it should be close to the results and not interrupt them.

### Two new ideas

**Magic Filter** is a button on the product listing, for users who have ordered at least once. Based on behaviour, previous orders and store type, it applies filters the user can then customise. The Peak-End Rule suggests this could be the experience's peak. It's only a seed-stage concept.

**The detailed product filter** borrows a familiar pattern from B2C sites such as Amazon and Alibaba, but personalised.

### What I learned

Counting the evaluation changed how I saw the product. The problems looked like many small issues, but most came down to consistency and recognition.

### What I'd do next

Fix bulk checkout, make the human face and Buy Again label clearer, and keep researching Magic Filter.
